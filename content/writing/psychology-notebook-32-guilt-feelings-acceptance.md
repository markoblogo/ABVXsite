_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

Acceptance is an antidote to guilt.

---

Politeness and rules of conduct can stand in for emotional self-regulation that has not been sufficiently developed.

---

Guilt as a way of avoiding the encounter with being unnecessary in the family system.

---

Manic defence is a wild dance to the accompaniment of guilt, distracting us from the horror of loss.

---

Guilt is proportional to the sense of your own omnipotence. The way out of self-blame lies in relinquishing omnipotence.

---

The most important critical judgement concerns your own state. Attacking yourself is not critical reflection; it is self-directed aggression.

---

Sometimes we need to construct a negative character outside ourselves so that we can feel good by comparison.

---

When you begin to see the pain behind behaviour, it becomes much easier to relate to people.

---

“How many passive-aggressive people does it take to change a light bulb?”

“As if you don’t know.”

---

Emotional vampirism is not calculated manipulation, but a weak internal economy: an inability to exchange and a constant dependence on donors.

---

Dependency is a way of meeting needs and regulating emotions through merging with a substance, person or game, rather than doing so autonomously.

---

Character can help you overcome obstacles and pursue your own course. But weak emotional self-regulation is often passed off as character.

---

Our perception of a situation is always shaped by our internal context. If that context rests on emotional pain and woundedness, even a relatively neutral situation can feel destructive and unbearable.

---

One important sign of emotional maturity is stability in the face of feedback. Another person’s response may be unpleasant, but it will not be devastating.

---

Disrupted emotional regulation reduces adaptability: unregulated emotional states consume a great deal of energy and do little to help communication and interaction with others.

---

Without any stress, we become fragile and unstable. With too much stress, we become exhausted and weak. Adaptation means finding a balance so that what activates us does not begin to destroy us.

---

Dependency often develops around difficulty recognising your own emotions. Any emotion, pleasant or unpleasant, may be interpreted as craving: a signal that it is time to take something.

---

Banished feelings later return with reinforcements from their opposite. Banished rage, for example, returns with apathy in support.

---

Curiously, the more a person is preoccupied with guilt, the less able they are to apologise and forgive.

---

Emotions carry information. They matter for understanding ourselves and the world. But a strong emotional reaction to a familiar situation may indicate that adaptation has not occurred and the situation is still ranked as dangerous and destructive.
