_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

What distinguishes the author’s position is taking responsibility for how the story unfolds and how it ends.

---

Wringing your hands over the meaning of life can be an excuse to do nothing. “If someone would hand me a meaning, just you wait and see what I’d do!”

---

It’s more pleasant to think about the injustice of the world and your unrecognized genius than about your own laziness.

---

Reality is hard to acknowledge because then you have to act in line with what is real, instead of what you dreamed or imagined.

---

Some people find it easier to adapt to necessity than to freedom. Freedom is pure uncertainty. Duty and obligations are another matter.

---

“I don’t know. Nothing can be changed. What can I possibly decide?” Few things take more effort than resisting a move out of passivity.

---

Life is movement. Living things are moved by the world around them and by their own will. If circumstances are the only thing moving you, it may be worth thinking about.

---

Why is it sometimes so hard to keep going after a failure? Because the failure feels like a catastrophe for the whole self instead of one part of the experience.

---

The character asks, “Why does life keep doing this to me?” The author asks, “What do I keep doing that makes this scene play out again?”

---

People live in a way that works for them. When it stops working, they start doing something about it. If they do nothing, it still works well enough for them to keep going. Of course, some circumstances are beyond our control. What we do with those circumstances is a choice.
