_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

Belittling something can be a response to danger. Once I’ve cut it down to size, it no longer feels quite so threatening.

---

Keeping your inner space in order matters for your well-being. When negative thoughts, attacks on yourself and things you haven’t worked through pile up, it’s hard to feel well.

---

The less solid your sense of self, the more you depend on other people’s opinions and judgments.

---

How other people treat you is only part of the question. The main question is how you treat yourself. The way you answer it shapes the people you choose to have around you.

---

It helps to have a kindly inner voice to talk to and a steady vantage point from which to observe yourself. The first lets you answer inner attacks. The second gives you some perspective when it’s time to be critical.

---

The jester’s role: if I control the laughter and decide who gets mocked, no one can touch me.

---

We often treat our own pain the way our parents treated us.

---

You are the person trying to live with the wound and deal with it. The wound itself isn’t you.

---

An easily offended person may already think poorly of themselves. What stings is hearing someone else confirm what they already think. It hurts!

---

When there’s danger, the inner critic may get harsher and sharper. If that was the tone of your whole childhood, your mind is recreating the feeling of being “at home.”
