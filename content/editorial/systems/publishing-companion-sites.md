# Publishing Companion Sites for Book Series and Editions

A retailer listing is designed around a purchase decision. A publishing companion site can do more: explain the idea behind a series, connect related editions, offer reader resources, document a language or provide a place to continue exploring after a book. It gives a publishing line a public home that can organize information around the reader's questions.

The ABVX catalogue includes companion sites for [Chinese Wisdom in toki pona](/work/chinese-wisdom-toki-pona-landing), [Stoic Wisdom in Toki Pona](/work/stoic-wisdom-toki-pona-landing), [Ukrainian Modernism](/work/ukrainian-modernism-landing) and [Toki Pona Free Kits](/work/toki-pona-free-kits-landing), as well as a home for the [Pictiq](/work/pictiq-landing) visual protocol. Each project has its own subject and audience; the shared pattern is a site that supports a publication rather than repeating a store page.

## What a companion site can provide

A useful book companion page can orient a new reader, show how a series fits together, explain a translation or format, and link to free guides or related work. For an experimental edition, it can introduce the language and notation used in the book. For a multi-book line, it can make reading order and relationships easier to follow.

The site should clarify what is available and where a link leads. It should not imply that a free resource is a paid edition or that an experimental language protocol is a universal standard. Accurate descriptions help readers choose the right next step.

## Publishing as an ongoing system

Dedicated sites make it possible to update context, add resources and preserve a stable route to a project over time. They also create links among books, language tools and editorial material that would otherwise be scattered.
