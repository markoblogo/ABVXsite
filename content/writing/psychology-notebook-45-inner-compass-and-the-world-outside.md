_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

Our inner image of well-being is like a compass used to steer the ship of our fate. If that image is blurred, unattractive, borrowed from someone else or simply unappealing, the ship drifts on the waves instead of following a course:

‘Perhaps I will be washed up somewhere that feels good and suits me?’

---

The way we imagine ourselves and the world is the way we build our strategy for adaptation.

---

Maladaptation arises where our description of reality and what is actually happening diverge sharply. This does not always mean that something bad is happening while our description says everything is wonderful, although that too is maladaptive because it overestimates things and shuts its eyes to problems. Sometimes what is happening is fine, but the description is ‘horror, horror’. Adaptation is disrupted because the psyche does not accept what is good and instead reacts to a feeling that things are bad.

---

Our inner state depends on external circumstances, but is not wholly determined by them. The idea that changing external circumstances is guaranteed to change our inner state is appealing, but does not work. Our inner state is shaped by the agreement we have with ourselves, kindness towards ourselves, healed psychological wounds, and our general health. External circumstances can affect how we feel: access to material and social resources does have a positive effect on mood and well-being. Yet many people in the world have resources and still feel middling.

Betting everything on the external world inevitably means losing.

---

Things do not get worse because you know yourself better.
