_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

It can be hard to give up suffering, because you’re also giving up the idea that the world owes you something for it.

---

Grieving is a kind of bargain. For the pain to ease, you gradually have to stop counting on what you lost and imagining you can still do something with it.

---

While you’re busy looking for someone to blame, you can’t face the full weight of the loss and begin to grieve. Blame keeps grief at a distance.

---

It’s hard to say goodbye to the past because what you hold on to are the experiences people and things gave you.

---

Feeling for someone who has lost something and trying to grieve harder than they do are two different things. The latter is a way of claiming the part of the person who suffered without suffering the loss.

---

Regret wears you out by playing the same “if only, then…” record again and again. Regret is a natural part of grieving. We can’t let go of a loss at once. But endless regret leaves us stuck inside the loss.

---

Devaluing what you’ve lost can be a way of avoiding grief.

---

Putting off grief is an attempt to keep what’s lost in the real world. If I don’t experience the loss or acknowledge it, it still hasn’t happened.

---

“I wasn’t finished!” The need to have the last word keeps the past from becoming the past. It leaves the past on the list of things you still plan to do. There are no unfinished gestalts, only ones whose endings didn’t sit right with you.

---

Someone who is anxious may find a loss easier to bear than a threat. A loss brings certainty. With a threat, you may still be able to prevent the loss. The trouble is deciding what to do to minimize it when everything is uncertain.
