_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

You walk over, sit down in the chair, look across and ask without words: ‘What is it like living inside yourself?’

---

Unmanageable debts can be a sign of an unrealistic assessment of yourself and excessive impulsiveness.

---

Emotional maturity is essentially the ability to regulate your own emotions without constantly looking for an external regulator.

---

Someone asked how to stop devaluing people. Well, start by imagining that you are not trying to strip someone outside you of their worth: through that devaluation, you are trampling on yourself.

---

Feeling deprived as a way of giving yourself more rights and fewer obligations. I have suffered and put up with so much that I am now allowed more than other people, and they owe me more than I owe them.

---

Everything is fine with the consumer position, except that the consumer feels bad. A subject is always happy, whereas a consumer is always being used. Used by themselves, at that.

---

Hostility requires polarisation. Arguments sustain polarisation; empathy reduces it.
‘A person has no enemies but themselves.’

---

An adult is someone who can distinguish themselves within the endless stream of experiences and sensations.

---

Anxious people calm down when there is danger. Like: everything is just as I thought.
If there is no danger, anxious people invent it for themselves. To calm down.

---

A loser easily finds a place in a dictatorship because a run of failures is usually tied to an external locus of control, while a dictatorship rests on the illusion that someone else knows better.

---

The fear of being left and abandoned is self-objectification. You can discard a little object; you cannot discard a subject, because a subject is always one, and even when beside someone, is beside another one.

---

Being unable to do anything without an external observer who evaluates you and tells you what to do is like having a faulty system for getting yourself going.

---

The skill of turning experiences into a coherent story and telling it brings considerable relief in itself.

---

Self-esteem is not the same as self-worth. Self-esteem means knowing your actual capabilities and abilities. Self-worth is the ability to value yourself rather than assess yourself: for existing, not for achievements.

---

Suffering in love is built on being able to tell yourself that happiness was so possible while you are being rejected, pushed away or simply met with indifference.

---

Until you know how to treat yourself well, the hope that someone else’s good treatment will break the spell and fix things is doomed to fail.

---

You can only abandon someone who has no self. Otherwise, it is not abandonment but parting and gradually living through the loss.

---

Repeating stories are an attempt to replay the first story in which we felt pain. The trouble is that you cannot replay it differently while playing the very same role.

---

External evaluations matter where the sense of self is very weak. Either I score 110 out of 100, or I simply do not exist.

---

Being drawn into a parental role means trying to become not even the parent the person you look after apparently needs, but the parent you needed yourself.
