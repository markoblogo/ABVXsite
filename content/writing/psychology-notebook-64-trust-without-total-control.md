_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

You cannot magically influence someone into feeling safe. You can provide conditions on your side—reliability, empathy, tenderness, compassion and recognition of the person's unconditional dignity, revealed in their suffering—and invite them to trust and rely on that.

---

Trust is not naivety or stupidity. It is a good filter for the people around you, combined with confidence that, if someone lets you down, you will be able to deal with the consequences.

---

The question is not how to remain constantly balanced. Constant balance can lead to rigidity and narrow the range of responses to unforeseen circumstances. The question is how quickly you can return from imbalance to balance.

---

The belief that inner problems and a negative attitude towards yourself can be solved by external circumstances, magical techniques, spiritual practices, a special person with precious resources or a change of location is, first, an unwillingness to encounter pain in order to begin healing an inner wound. Second, it is a belief in the existence of an ultimate saviour.

---

The more a person tries to overcome their vulnerability, the more vulnerable they become. Adaptability includes accepting that it is impossible to cope with everything.

---

Trying to buy your way out of what you dislike in another person's behaviour or experience does not work: it positively reinforces negative behaviour.

---

An emotional safety margin is a cushion that softens difficult situations, giving you time to experience and rethink them. The smaller that cushion, the harder it is to face even moderately intense emotional events, let alone truly difficult situations.

---

Adaptability is the ability to switch from tension to relaxation. If there is nothing but tension, sooner or later overexcitement turns into exhaustion. If there is nothing but relaxation, the result is sluggishness, laziness and loss of conditioning.

---

Our adult part is responsible for testing reality and regulating behaviour and experience in the present. In essence, it manages resources in the moment. Emotional self-regulation is not the repression of self-expression, but an assessment of the inner economy: do I currently have the strength for an emotional somersault, or would it cost too much?

---

When we are in danger, instantly deciding who is an enemy and dividing the world into black and white help us survive. But if the danger has passed and the colours other than black and white have not returned, that gets in the way of living.
