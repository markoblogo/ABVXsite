_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

Staying in an unbearable relationship because you have already been together so long, and how could you leave it all, reminds me of a story. A guy comes to a poker club and says, ‘Teach me to play.’ They listen and talk to him, and find a complete failure of elementary arithmetic and logic. They tell him, ‘Maybe try something other than poker.’ He replies, ‘How can I stop playing poker? I have already lost a hundred thousand. I need to win it back.’

---

The foundation of romantic drama is the belief that, if you love someone properly, they will inevitably become the person you need them to be.

---

Joy and shared values matter in a couple. Without joy, it is unclear why there is a couple. Without shared values, there will be chronic conflict.

---

In adulthood, ‘I truly love you’ can be tested by one thing: how the person saying it makes your life better. If they do not, or make it worse, or make it better in their imagination while you feel worse, that is not love but bedtime stories.

---

The barriers to closeness are not physical features, but fear and avoidance of closeness. It is easier to say, ‘I never get together with anyone because there is something wrong with my legs,’ than, ‘I never get together with anyone because closeness involves risk and frustration.’

---

What a person with a demonstrative disposition reads as interest is often simply irritation.

---

Swinging between idealization and devaluation keeps you relating to someone as an object. A subject cannot be either entirely good or entirely bad.

---

Idealization as a defence is a way of avoiding reality in order to reduce anxiety.

The person idealizing someone feels such anxiety about living interaction that they construct that perfect image, which seems to absorb all the anxiety.

It is particularly pleasant to idealize what is inaccessible: the more inaccessible it is, the longer you can avoid encountering your actual fears and worries.

---

Relationships that are painful but that you do not leave consist of bait and a hook.

The bait is what draws you into the relationship: the hope of what it might offer. For some, that is attention; for others, recognition; perhaps it is a potential resource that the partner seems able to provide.

The hook is the pain you experience when trying to leave. It might be fear, helplessness, falling self-esteem or an attempt to prevent a self-fulfilling prophecy.

The sweeter and more tempting the bait, the more painfully and deeply you remain hooked.

---

Closeness means being able to see another person realistically while not hiding or endlessly censoring yourself. Mutual acceptance matters in closeness.

Closeness provides strength and helps us cope with difficulties.

Attachment where there is no real closeness is self-deception and getting stuck in illusions.
