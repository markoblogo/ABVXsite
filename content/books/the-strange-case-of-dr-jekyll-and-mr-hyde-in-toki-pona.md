## **The Strange Case of Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde in Toki Pona**

What changes when a familiar story is read through a language built from a deliberately small vocabulary? This edition of Robert Louis Stevenson’s *The Strange Case of Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde* invites readers to find out—not by replacing the English original, but by placing it beside a Toki Pona translation and its sitelen pona rendering.

The book presents the story in three connected layers: Stevenson’s English text, the translation in Latin-script Toki Pona, and sitelen pona—the writing system used to represent that same Toki Pona text. The glyphs are not a separate translation. They give readers another way to follow and experience the Toki Pona layer.

## **A literary classic, read three ways**

The layered format makes this more than a conventional reprint or a standalone translation. Readers can follow the English, pause over how an idea is expressed in Toki Pona, and then encounter that wording in sitelen pona. Moving between the layers can make familiar passages feel newly open to interpretation: a compact phrase may focus attention on one aspect of an idea while leaving another implicit.

That experience is central to the edition. Toki Pona’s small vocabulary does not simply make the story shorter or easier; it changes the work of reading. Meaning depends on context, word choice and the relationships between ideas. Comparing the English and Toki Pona passages lets readers notice those choices for themselves.

## **What is sitelen pona?**

Sitelen pona is a writing system for Toki Pona. In this edition, it provides a visual rendering of the Toki Pona translation alongside its Latin-script form. Readers who already know sitelen pona can use it as an additional reading layer. Those new to it can see how the written forms relate to the Latin-script text, without needing to treat the symbols as a new English-to-glyph translation.

## **Who might enjoy this edition?**

This Toki Pona edition of *Jekyll and Hyde* is intended for readers curious about Toki Pona, sitelen pona, constructed languages and the choices involved in literary translation. It may also interest readers who know Stevenson’s story and want to approach it through a different linguistic lens.

It is a reading edition, not a step-by-step Toki Pona course. Its purpose is to let readers compare the original, the translation and the visual writing layer as they move through a complete literary work.

*The Strange Case of Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde — Toki Pona Edition with Sitelen Pona* brings English literature, minimalist language and visual reading together in one layered book.
