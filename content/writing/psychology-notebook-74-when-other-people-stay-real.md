_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

Treating people as useful objects leaves you starved for real contact. When you use others, you use yourself too.

---

Real intimacy asks you to know and accept each other in your imperfections. Demanding a perfect partner won’t get you there.

---

Idealizing and devaluing someone can both keep intimacy at a distance. In idealization, the imagined person hides the real one. In devaluation, coming closer brings on disgust.

---

Openness makes closeness possible and exposes you to being hurt. Building resilience and learning to cope can give someone who’s been hurt a chance at intimacy.

---

In a relationship, power belongs to the person who needs less.

---

The more you fear loss, the harder it is to get close to someone.

---

Attachment rests on being able to bear separation from someone you love without forgetting them, pushing them out of mind or cursing them for being away.

---

Fearful-avoidant attachment: “I like this. It feels good and suits me. Right, then. It’s a trap. Run!”

---

The Snow Queen is Gerda after she decides it would be more fun to hang out on the ice than to drag Kai back home.

---

In destructive relationships, the unpredictable reward can have a stronger hold than love. Things might be good tomorrow, in a month or never, and the waiting creates its own suspense and unhealthy attachment.
