_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

A computer has no unconscious.

---

Critical situations expose the desert of the real. It becomes clear that many of our ideas about ourselves and the world are merely coverings laid over that desert.

---

Our adaptability depends on our picture of the world. The poorer and more schematic that picture, the less adaptable we are. Overly rigid ideas about the world also reduce adaptability: either things match the picture in my head, or—aaah, catastrophe! Something is not going my way. A threat. The red zone of stress.

---

In life, we need to balance typology and phenomenology. Constantly digging into the particularities of something typical means failing to classify, generalize and notice patterns. Seeing nothing but types everywhere makes for a dreary life: apparently, you already have this whole world figured out.

---

Why is agency a new luxury?

Agency resists impersonality. It is not an imitation of originality, but having your own outlook and goals that you are prepared to defend and express through the course of your life.

Yet expressing yourself this way always carries the risk of being neither accepted nor understood. Being an agent of your own life means being able to withstand those risks.

---

The idea that the world should become a place that suits you is tempting, but impossible to realize. Accepting that the world existed before you, will exist after you and has somehow always managed is an important part of growing up.

---

Life is dynamic. Anxiety appears when, instead of accepting its movement, changeability and mystery, we begin demanding that life stand still and become understandable and predictable. Interestingly, when an anxious person gains a temporary illusion of a world standing still, they do not settle down: they begin suffering from boredom.

---

Self-confidence does not mean considering yourself right. Being right all the time is not confidence, but distorted perception. Confidence means being able to acknowledge mistakes when you encounter them, rather than becoming fixated on how bad you are.

---

Having principles is a good quality while it stays within a moderate range. Life usually punishes a lack of principles. With an excessively rigid system of principles, people start punishing themselves, and become inflexible under stress.

---

People tend not to notice what falls outside their life script. If the script says that everyone will deceive and abandon you, then out of a hundred stories of support, you will notice only the one involving deception.
