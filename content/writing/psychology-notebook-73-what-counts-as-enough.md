_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

When there’s a whole vast, astonishing world around you, taking an interest only in yourself is a recipe for a thoroughly dull life.

---

A good description of how I’m doing: I’m living without needless effort.

---

Often we want what’s familiar more than we want what’s good.

---

A pull toward extremes can mean we’ve lost touch with the feeling of having enough.

---

What you want in abundance now is often what you once had too little of: attention, love, a sense of being valued.

---

Suffering can give the impression that something is happening even if you’re doing nothing but lying there feeling sorry for yourself.

---

A recipe for suffering: think more about what’s missing and take less notice of what’s already there.

---

It can be hard to give up the role of the person who always fails when failure earns affection and approval.

---

Change in adulthood always starts with a shortfall, rather than a surplus.

---

The idea that you’ll be happy only if life goes entirely your way runs into reality. First, it won’t go entirely your way. Second, getting exactly what you wanted often leaves you bruised instead of fulfilled.
