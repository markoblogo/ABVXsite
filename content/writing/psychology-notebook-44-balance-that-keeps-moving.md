_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

Tolerance of uncertainty largely determines both immediate and long-term adaptation.

---

Adaptation depends on a broad repertoire of responses, rather than one thoroughly learned adaptive role model.

---

An **adaptive repertoire** is the full range of ways someone uses to cope with change, stress and the demands of their environment.

A broad repertoire means flexibility: a person can hide, attack, negotiate or leave. They do not get stuck in one strategy.

A limited repertoire is like playing the same note your entire life. For example, only ‘attack’ or only ‘freeze’. It is as though a theatre were staging The Nutcracker, but you could only play a little bear and wondered why you had not been cast. Such strategies can be effective, but only in a limited number of situations. In others, they reduce adaptability.

---

Adaptation is a dynamic process. You cannot find your balance once and remain frozen in it forever.

Without any balance, there will be maladaptation. But trying to freeze in a balance you have found also leads to maladaptation.

---

Adaptation depends not only on how we cope, but also on how we experience situations we cannot cope with. Life is such that coping with everything is impossible. Giving up the idea of omnipotence gives us a chance to live.

---

To adapt is to be on the side of life.
