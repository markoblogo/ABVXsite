_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

Transference:

“Is that you, Mum/Dad?”

Countertransference:

“Darling, it’s me!”

---

The opposite of dependency is not so much freedom as presence and wholeness.

---

Separation from your parents is necessary, if only so that you do not follow them when they go.

---

Dependency is revenge on the world for being unable to become a mother.

---

A challenge with a star: find a bad boy and turn him into a good dad.

---

Internal conflict: I want to be a baby, and for the whole world to be an attentive mother.

---

Whatever you depend on is your imagined mother. But dependency is a mother who lulls you to death.

---

The codependent person services someone else’s pain to avoid encountering their own.

---

The ability to receive criticism calmly, judge your own state adequately and reflect are all signs of emotional maturity.

---

As long as accusations against our parents remain, we cannot see them as people rather than failed functions.

---

For a child raised in a family with dependencies, avoiding dependency — including dependence in relationships — can become a kind of dependency itself.

---

Interestingly, people often come to therapy talking about their parents at roughly the age their parents were in their memories.

---

There is a great deal of energy in the childlike position. Wonderful. The problem is that this position is childlike in its thinking and emotional regulation too.

---

A pronounced character develops in response to circumstances. At some point it begins to create circumstances of its own, and they are not always pleasant.

---

The less a boy can express aggression towards his mother before and during puberty, the harder it is afterwards to relinquish the idea of marrying her.

---

If someone is deeply upset about the parents they had, it indicates that they still need a parent: some part of the psyche has not been able to grow up.

---

The infant’s position is the most powerful: reality, in the shape of the mother, must answer the cry and read the needs. Remaining helpless is an attempt to preserve and restore that infantile absolute monarchy.

---

The first step in working with dependency is to see it not as your badness, but as an available way of coping with your pain.

---

Adaptability is impossible without autonomy. While someone in a parental position can meet your needs, and you remain completely dependent on outside circumstances, resilience to stress has nowhere to develop.

---

Closing your eyes to difficulties resembles the early-childhood idea that if you cannot see something, it cannot see you, and covering your face with your hands is enough to disappear.
