_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

Trying at any cost to get something from a person who is unwilling to give it is a form of violence.

---

There’s no point explaining yourself where people have no intention of understanding you.

---

Ignoring your limits lets you feel omnipotent right up until bankruptcy.

---

Everyone is entitled to their own delusions. Everyone is also entitled to decline the job of keeping someone else’s delusions going.

---

The urge to rescue someone comes from doubting that they can cope. And rescuing often gets in the way of their learning to rely on themselves.

---

Someone who gives everyone everything and keeps nothing for themselves may be trying to buy people off and purchase a little freedom from their obligations.

---

Saying no can express care for yourself without being unkind to the other person. Pressuring someone to do what doesn’t work for them is a form of violence.

---

When someone in therapy learns to say no, set limits and listen to themselves, the people around them may think, “We were all getting along just fine. Now therapy has made them rather unpleasant.”

---

Being able to tolerate frustration helps you get things done, provided you also ask whether that frustration is necessary. Simply enduring something you could do without gets you nowhere.

---

Separation means acknowledging the other person’s right to have their own feelings, including negative ones, and trusting that they can cope with them.
