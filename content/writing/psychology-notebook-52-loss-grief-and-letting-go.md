_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

One obstacle to beginning the work of grief is often the fear of giving up one's omnipotence. Acknowledging a loss means encountering one's vulnerability and weakness.

---

Grief reflects the value of what has been lost. In genuine closeness, we always sign a contract that includes the possibility of loss and grief.

---

A sign that a loss has been mourned is a deep gratitude that what was lost was once in your life.

---

To devalue something is to reduce the possible pain of losing what is valuable.

---

Fear protects us from sadness. While there is a threat, there is no need to mourn the loss.

---

I think that knowing good does not always defeat evil, and light does not always dispel darkness, can bring freedom after the initial despair.

---

The stronger the pain, the more exclusively you hear its commands. The voice of the wound can drown out any logic.

---

An unmourned loss makes any new undertaking enormously costly.

---

Guilt is the uppermost layer of grief.

---

While you are tormented by the question “Why is all this happening to me?”, it is hard to begin experiencing the loss. Even if someone suddenly told you “because of this and that”, it would solve nothing: you would still have to mourn what was lost.
