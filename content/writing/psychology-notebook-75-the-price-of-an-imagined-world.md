_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

Love dramas are often built on elaborate fantasies about what someone would surely have done for the person who’s suffering, even though they never intended to do anything of the sort.

---

You are where your thoughts are. No matter how good things are around you, if your head is hell, that’s where you live.

---

An open emotional wound is like a distorting pane of glass. It keeps you from seeing what’s there. Everything you look at through it comes back as pain.

---

Unhappy, unrequited love is a kind of self-hypnosis. You stop seeing what’s actually happening and start seeing your own fantasy projected onto the other person.

---

You pay for a life in the imagination with your real life.

---

“If I’m good enough, everything in the world will go my way!”

---

Living in illusions is sweet, but the longer you stay, the higher the rent.

---

Romantic suffering can give you the impression that you’re less alone. While you’re absorbed in someone’s image, you almost feel as though you have company.

---

Sometimes control is a form of hope: “If I think everything through carefully enough, reality will go the way I need it to.”

---

Magical thinking can look like an attempt to keep every threat under control: if you think through everything that might go wrong, you imagine you’re preventing it from happening.
