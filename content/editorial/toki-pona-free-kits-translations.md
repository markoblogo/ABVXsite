# **Toki Pona Free Kits & Translations**

Not every book I publish needs to be sold.

As my toki pona publishing catalogue grew, I wanted there to be a simple way for someone to enter it without first buying a book, choosing a series or even being completely sure that reading philosophy in toki pona was something they wanted to do.

That is why I created **Toki Pona Free Kits & Translations**.

It is a small collection of free books, translations and companion materials designed as an entry point into the larger toki pona publishing ecosystem.

You can download something, start reading and decide what to do next.

No commitment is required.

## **Learning by reading**

There are many ways to learn toki pona.

My own preference is to begin using a language as early as possible rather than spending too much time memorising vocabulary in isolation.

Toki pona is especially well suited to this.

Its vocabulary is intentionally small, words repeat frequently, and a short philosophical or literary text can expose the reader to the same basic structures again and again.

So the free materials are built around actual reading.

Read a few lines.

Notice the words that repeat.

Return to the same passage.

Compare different representations.

Gradually, things that initially required conscious decoding begin to become familiar.

The goal is not to replace a proper toki pona course. It is to give the reader real texts to practise with.

## **The Toki Pona Reader’s Kit**

The most straightforward place to begin is **The Toki Pona Reader’s Kit**.

It grew out of my *Stoic Wisdom in Toki Pona* publishing line and uses the **Golden Verses of Pythagoras** as a compact philosophical reading text.

The kit is designed both for reading and for repeated practice.

You can work with the Latin-script toki pona version and then use the same material as a bridge toward **sitelen pona**, the visual writing system commonly used with toki pona.

I wanted it to function as something between a book and a learning exercise.

You do not need to finish it once and put it away.

You can return to the same pages repeatedly until the language starts feeling less like something being translated in your head and more like something you simply read.

## **Chinese Wisdom**

The second free entry point comes from the **Chinese Wisdom in Toki Pona** series.

That publishing line includes works such as the *Dao De Jing* and *The Art of War*, but I also wanted one complete philosophical text to remain freely available.

For that I chose **Mozi’s Universal Love**.

It introduces another side of classical Chinese thought while using the same general approach as the paid books: minimalist translation, parallel reading and an emphasis on inspecting ideas rather than racing through the text.

It can also serve as a preview of the wider Chinese Wisdom series before deciding whether to explore the other books.

## **The Heart Sutra**

The **Heart Sutra** is another free translation.

It does not formally belong to the Chinese philosophy series, but it sits naturally beside it.

I have had a long personal interest in Zen Buddhism, and the Heart Sutra is an especially interesting text to translate into toki pona because both the source and the target language demand an unusual degree of compression.

A very small number of words is expected to carry a very large amount of meaning.

The edition combines several ways of approaching the text, including the source, reference material, toki pona and a sitelen pona-oriented representation.

For me, this is exactly the kind of text that benefits from slow, repeated reading rather than being treated as something to finish once.

## **Free books as part of publishing**

I try to include at least one free book or substantial free resource in many of my publishing series.

This is not only a marketing sample.

It is part of how I think publishing should work, particularly when the subject itself has a learning curve.

If I am asking someone to read Marcus Aurelius in toki pona, explore Chinese philosophy through sitelen pona, or experiment with a minimalist constructed language, it seems reasonable to provide a way to understand what that experience is like before asking them to purchase anything.

A free book lowers the first barrier.

It can also remain useful long after someone has bought other books in the series.

That is why these materials are not reduced previews containing a few promotional pages. I prefer them to be actual texts that can be read, studied and returned to.

## **Part of a larger toki pona ecosystem**

The free library is only one part of a much larger system I have gradually built around toki pona.

There are paid book series such as **Stoic Wisdom in Toki Pona** and **Chinese Wisdom in Toki Pona**.

There is an online translator that can transform text from ordinary languages into toki pona and related visual representations.

There are learning materials, sitelen pona conventions, publishing protocols and tools for representing toki pona on websites.

Some of my sites can themselves be read in toki pona, sitelen pona or emoji pona through tools I developed for that purpose.

The free books sit in the middle of this ecosystem.

They connect learning with publishing.

Someone might discover toki pona through a free Reader’s Kit and later read Marcus Aurelius.

Someone interested in the *Dao De Jing* might encounter sitelen pona for the first time.

Someone might arrive through the Heart Sutra and end up experimenting with the translator or another language project.

There is no required path.

## **A low-friction way to begin**

The purpose of **Toki Pona Free Kits & Translations** is ultimately simple.

If toki pona interests you, start reading.

If sitelen pona looks intriguing, try it.

If the idea of compressing philosophy into a tiny language sounds strange, take one of the texts and see what happens.

You do not need to buy anything first.

The free collection exists so that the first step can simply be curiosity.
