_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

Moderation matters in the ways we replenish our resources: in excess, a channel can drain rather than replenish them. For example, too much social interaction will exhaust rather than satisfy. Fantasy and imagination can provide resources, but excessive fantasizing does not replenish a person: it removes them from the outside world.

---

Taking care of yourself does not mean indulging every impulse. Self-care is more often about saying no than saying yes. No to unpleasant company. No to bad habits. No to what does not suit you. No to what takes more energy than it gives. That is how care works.

---

Seeing another person in their difficulties, fears, doubts and despair is hard. Sometimes unbearable. Where we cannot bear someone else's difficulties, we want to rescue them: supposedly the other person, but actually ourselves, resonating with their pain and helplessness. Sometimes saying no to our urge to inflict goodness and impose happiness is how we say yes to another person's strength. And avoid robbing them through our rescuing.

---

You can tell how a person is adapting by how they look.

So reproaching someone for looking too fat, too grey, too yesterday, too dreary or too anything else is rather like saying: you could be doing a better job of adapting.

---

Many religious traditions count pride as a sin because it leads to fixation on oneself and prevents us from seeing the wider world around us.

---

Playing down other people's ability to cope is a way of feeling important.

---

Failing to follow your own decision out of pity for another person essentially harms both of you. You harm yourself by doing something that does not suit you at all, because it offers an illusory sense of superiority over someone pitiable. It harms the other person too: you create conditions in which they need not develop independence, and can use someone else's resources simply because they are supposedly so weak and pitiable.

---

When we do not give a realistic account of what does not suit us in a relationship, trying to spare someone additional frustration, both sides end up frustrated. One lives under a deception; the other lives in discontent.

---

To be unbearable and still have people around you requires a significant resource. Money, abilities, attractiveness and magnetism, special skills and knowledge are all possible resources. If those resources are absent or have been squandered, and someone continues being unbearable, that is a way of arranging their own solitude.

---

How do you give up something you love when it is destroying you? Only by acknowledging that the damage is much greater than what the love provides.
