_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

A person sometimes gets stuck in whichever emotion their family used to respond to. In some families it was tears, in others rage; some encouraged joy but did not welcome sadness.

---

Behind the shame of being unable to be perfect lies a strong need for love.
- Maybe if I do become perfect, they will love me.

---

Shame is like a little death: I wish I could disappear.

---

Shame is connected with death: freezing, slowing down so much that you wish the ground would swallow you.

---

Touchiness is malignant egocentrism.

---

Guilt is the currency of those who cannot grieve in order to let go.

---

Sadness expresses the value of what has been lost.

---

Fear is the most activating emotion. So being afraid can sometimes be like having a little coffee.

---

The past affects the present not as cruel fate or predestination. The more emotions remain unprocessed in the past, the more the past is our present.

---

Guilt as a way of understanding relationships: either I am guilty, or others are guilty towards me.

---

Shame and loneliness form a duet: shame makes you want to disappear, and once you disappear, loneliness kicks in.

---

Sadness alone is not enough to grieve. After sadness, it is important to say goodbye to the loss and let go, rather than constantly returning to a new cycle of grief.

---

Resentment is a fantasy about how things should have been instead of living through how they were.

---

Resentment is aggression that has no rights, which makes it even more destructive than aggression that does have rights.

---

The inner critic can be active, aggressive and angry; then it is easy to notice. It is harder when the critic is quiet but frightening, full of shame and disappointment.

---

The worse someone’s emotional self-regulation, the harder individual and group interaction becomes. When you are still young and cute, people may tolerate weak regulation of affect; later, of course, difficulties begin.

---

Emotional instability is one of the main factors reducing the ability to adapt. Emotional swings are a wonderful fairground ride when you are young, but a very costly pastime at a more mature age.

---

People who struggle to bear other people’s sadness usually cannot bear their own sadness at all.

---

Being able to encounter your own shadow and acknowledge it is a sign of emotional maturity. The more difficulty someone has regulating emotions, the more unambiguously bright an image of themselves they can bear.

---

Anxiety is guilt in advance.
