_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

Anxious thoughts give little things enormous shadows.

---

The level of aggression always corresponds to the inner sense of danger.

---

Rejecting anything good for fear of betraying the familiar bad.

---

Fear is a way of defeating boredom. Give yourself a fright, and things get interesting straight away.

---

Sometimes the fear of a possible loss is so strong that it paralyses the ability to act. Losing in advance so as not to lose.

---

For anxious people, fear is like coffee: it perks you up and helps you borrow strength from yourself.

---

Living without ever showing yourself means never really living, out of fear and shame.

---

De-idealisation happens when the idealised object can no longer contain all the repressed anxiety.

---

Anxiety is an unpleasant feeling, but sometimes it is a load-bearing structure that gives us at least some sense of life.

---

Fear of closeness as an expression of separation anxiety: coming together with somebody means leaving an imagined mother.

---

Sometimes a person feels deeply worried, but not about what is actually worth worrying about.

---

Listening to your heart is good when your mind is clear. When your mind is anxious, you listen to your heart and hear tachycardia.

---

Behind endless demands and complaints against the world lies fear. If the world is not perfect, it must be dangerous.

---

One reason for getting stuck in anxious thoughts is difficulty shifting attention. You think of something bad and get stuck there.

---

— I cannot live unless I anticipate everything. But if I anticipate everything, I do not live.

The paradoxes of obsessive thinking.

---

Panicking is the flight response in words. A panicker always spends more strength on panic than on overcoming the actual danger.

---

Bitterness reveals inner fear and a life lived under threat. If someone is at peace inside, why spend their strength on fury?

---

Sensitivity to criticism and a paralysing fear of possible judgement reveal a fragile self-image. As though other people's words could destroy it.

---

Fear of abandonment is self-objectification. An object can be discarded; a subject whose relationship ends may grieve, but can cope and go on living.

---

Avoiding freedom as a fear of abandonment and loneliness. If I do not depend on anyone and nobody depends on me, who needs me?
