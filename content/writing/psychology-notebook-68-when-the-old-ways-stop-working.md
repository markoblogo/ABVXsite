_A thematic selection of translated notebook fragments._

_Cover image supplied from the owner-authorized Psychology Notebook reserve._

To get through a crisis, you have to experience it, rather than try to hide or run from it. A crisis reveals the inner conflicts around which a personality is organized, and helps uncover new ways of reaching an internal truce.

---

The fight response is necessary when we face a serious challenge. Fighting matters when winning will remove the source of stress.

But when the fight response turns against the entire surrounding world, serious victories may never arrive: there will simply be no strength left for them.

---

It is bitter when a child does not experience adequate care or support and has no sense of safety. This leaves wounds. But an adult can learn self-support, self-care and the ability to hear their own needs, helping those wounds close so that, although scars remain, they no longer cause constant, unbearable pain.

---

Adaptability is largely determined by how well a person can preserve a sense of inner well-being despite external circumstances.

Inner well-being does not mean thoughtless optimism, but an interest in life and confidence in one's own strength.

---

Reappraising a situation can reduce its stressful weight. Seeing what happened as part of your experience, rather than as a negative judgement of yourself, helps you live through frustrations and failures while preserving energy for further change.

---

Immediate adaptability can be improved by knowing the possible responses to acute stress. There are no good or bad responses when encountering danger: there are adaptive and maladaptive strategies for coping with a threat.

---

Avoiding encounters with life, trying to protect yourself from the slightest challenge or danger, ultimately leads to loss of conditioning and substantially reduces resilience to stress.

---

Personality includes an adaptive repertoire. The child's position within that repertoire is: the world should adapt to my needs. The adult's position is: I can find ways of adapting to the most unexpected threats, losses and challenges in the surrounding world.

---

Adaptability is built into us by nature. If we are alive, that means thousands upon thousands of our ancestors managed to cope.

---

A crisis is a period when the old ways of adapting stop working and new ones have not yet formed.

Crises cannot be avoided. What matters is learning to move through them in a way that leads to a deeper understanding of yourself and the world.

A crisis is an interval, a transitional period towards something new.

Getting stuck in a crisis costs a great deal of energy. The economics of intervals are much more expensive than periods of stability. Constant crisis? Look for the interval you are stuck in, and the reasons you cannot get out.
