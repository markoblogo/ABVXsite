# Agro Market Infrastructure Systems for Physical Commodity Markets

Physical grain and oilseed markets depend on more than a quoted price. A commercial decision can involve quality, location, delivery terms, freight, currency, logistics, policy, crop conditions and the reliability of the information behind an offer. Agro market infrastructure systems make those moving parts easier to observe, compare and coordinate.

On ABVX, this ecosystem brings together software and public information surfaces for physical agro-commodity operations. It includes brokerage and trading workflows, market monitoring, commodity benchmarks, partner-facing sites and tools that explain how a product or market framework works. These are connected layers with different jobs, not one interchangeable product category.

## Four connected layers

Trading and brokerage platforms help teams keep transaction context, counterparties and execution steps together. [MN7R](/work/mn7r) focuses on the operating work around brokerage, while [Cropto](/work/cropto) explores indexed spot trading and related digital instruments.

Monitoring and intelligence systems organize signals that would otherwise remain scattered across market commentary, logistics, weather, policy and price references. [Cropto Monitor](/work/cropto-monitor), [Last30Days Cropto](/work/last30days-cropto) and the index projects [1D3X](/work/1d3x), [SPIKE](/work/spike-spot-commodity-index-ukraine) and [UGA Index](/work/uga-index) illustrate distinct approaches to market context and benchmarks.

Market fronts and landings translate these capabilities for readers, partners and prospective users. They give an infrastructure project a clear public explanation, a concrete entry point and links to the underlying service or methodology.

## Why the layers matter

A benchmark does not run a brokerage workflow, and a landing page does not replace either one. But clear relationships between these layers help people understand where an observation comes from, what a system is meant to do and how to follow up. The goal is practical legibility: turning fragmented market information into repeatable reference points, usable workflows and public context.

This catalogue is an overview of work in progress as well as established public surfaces. Each linked project has its own scope and status. Together, they show how software, data, interfaces and market practice can support better coordination in physical commodity markets.
