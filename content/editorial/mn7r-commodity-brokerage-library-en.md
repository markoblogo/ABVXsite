# **MN7R Commodity Brokerage Library**

The **MN7R Commodity Brokerage Library** grew out of practical work in physical commodity markets.

Most books about trading tend to focus on financial markets, speculation, charts, strategies or investment.

That is not what this library is about.

Physical commodity trading is a different kind of work.

A grain deal has to be found, negotiated, documented, financed, transported, monitored, executed and closed. Someone has to understand the product, the basis, the logistics, the counterparties, the documentation, the risks and what happens when something goes wrong between agreement and delivery.

The purpose of this publishing line is to document that operational layer.

## **From market knowledge to working knowledge**

A large part of commodity-market knowledge still lives in conversations.

Experienced brokers know things that are rarely written down.

Traders have their own spreadsheets.

Execution teams maintain their own checklists.

Logistics specialists know which details matter because they have already seen what happens when somebody misses them.

A new broker often learns through a mixture of observation, messages, calls and mistakes.

I wanted to make more of that knowledge explicit.

The books in this library are therefore not intended to explain commodities from a distance. They are designed around the actual workflow behind physical-market transactions.

How does a broker organise a deal?

What information matters before an offer becomes actionable?

How do logistics affect price?

What does basis mean in practice?

How does a transaction move from agreement to contract execution?

Which documents matter?

How do brokers, traders, execution officers, logistics teams and finance interact?

How can market data become something operational rather than merely informative?

These are the kinds of questions the library tries to answer.

## **Brokerage as a system**

The publishing line is closely connected to **MN7R**, the commodity brokerage workspace we are developing for modern brokerage teams.

MN7R is not simply a CRM for storing clients.

The larger idea is to connect clients, deals, market intelligence, execution, analytics, team workflows and supervised AI-assisted work inside one operating environment.

This creates an interesting relationship between software and publishing.

The software contains the workflow.

The books explain the workflow.

And the process of writing the books often exposes things that should be improved in the software.

The two evolve together.

That is why some titles in the library are living professional editions rather than books intended to remain unchanged forever.

## **Agro-Commodity Brokerage**

**Agro-Commodity Brokerage** is the most direct introduction to the brokerage side of this work.

It is a practical guide to grains, oilseeds, logistics, basis, deals, risk and team brokerage.

The purpose is not to teach someone how to speculate on commodity prices.

It is about the commercial work between market participants.

A broker has to understand far more than the number displayed as a price.

Where is the commodity?

Under which delivery terms?

What is the quality?

What logistics are available?

How reliable are the counterparties?

Is the price executable?

What happens after the parties say yes?

This is the operational reality the book tries to describe.

There are editions for both international and Ukrainian readers, because the underlying brokerage mechanics are global while the practical context can be very local.

## **Understanding the spot market**

The **Spot-Market Handbook** approaches the same world from another direction.

A spot market looks simple from the outside: buyers, sellers and a current price.

In practice, there is rarely one single price.

There are locations, delivery periods, qualities, bases, logistics, liquidity differences and constantly changing commercial conditions.

This becomes particularly important in agricultural markets.

Ukraine is a useful case because it is one of the world's significant agricultural producers and exporters, but the same underlying problem exists in many physical markets: how do you turn fragmented market information into a benchmark that people can actually use?

The handbook explores spot-market practice, market intelligence, respondent-based information, benchmarks, indices and the infrastructure required to transform market observations into usable commercial data.

This is closely connected to the market-monitoring and index systems I work on outside publishing as well.

## **Execution is part of the trade**

Another part of the library focuses on what happens **after the deal has been agreed**.

This is often underrepresented in discussions of trading.

Agreeing on a price is only one point in a much longer process.

A physical commodity contract has to move through contract formation, nominations, logistics, documentary control, payments, reconciliations and final closeout.

That is the focus of **The Contract Execution Officer Handbook**.

Execution officers sit at the intersection of commercial, logistical, documentary and financial work.

A transaction that looks profitable when it is agreed can become expensive very quickly if execution is poorly controlled.

For that reason, I see execution not as back-office administration but as part of the trading system itself.

## **Ukraine as a starting point, not a boundary**

A large part of my work in physical commodities begins with Ukraine.

That is natural.

I am Ukrainian, and Ukrainian agriculture remains an important part of the country's economy and its connection to international markets.

Ukraine is also an unusually rich environment for thinking about commodity-market infrastructure because so much depends on logistics, export routes, international counterparties, freight, ports, borders, changing risk and access to reliable information.

But the library is not intended to remain purely Ukrainian.

Brokerage, execution and physical-market infrastructure are international problems.

A grain broker in Ukraine, a trader in Europe and an execution team working with another agricultural origin may operate in different environments, but much of the underlying structure is recognisable.

That is why some books are Ukrainian, some are adapted for international audiences, and future editions can continue to expand beyond a single market.

## **Why most of the library is free**

Most of the professional material in this publishing line is available free of charge.

That is intentional.

These books are connected to the systems, market infrastructure and professional ecosystem we are building.

Their purpose is partly educational, but also infrastructural.

A better-informed broker is easier to work with.

A team that understands execution makes fewer avoidable mistakes.

A market participant who understands how benchmarks are created can use market data more intelligently.

And someone evaluating MN7R should be able to understand the ideas behind the product without first becoming a customer.

So I prefer to make much of this knowledge openly available in practical formats such as PDF, EPUB and, where appropriate, audio.
